## My pick: “Common Ground → Real Plans”

**A dating experience that helps people move from shared values to an actual, low-pressure date.**

Your positioning could be:

> **Meet someone who gets your world. Make a plan that fits both of you.**

That connects directly to Freeborn’s identity without making vaccination status or sensitive health information its entire identity.

### How the signature feature would work

**1. Show what shared values mean in everyday life.**

Rather than another list of labels, members answer a few optional, practical prompts:

- “My ideal ordinary Sunday looks like…”
- “In a relationship, personal freedom means…”
- “One lifestyle choice I’d want a partner to understand is…”
- “Something we can disagree about and still get along is…”

Members control which answers appear on their profile. **No fake compatibility percentage and no claim that matching answers guarantee chemistry.**

**2. Give a match a natural way to start talking.**

After both people like each other, either can invite the other to answer a **Common Ground question**.

For example:

> “You both enjoy being outdoors. What sounds better for a first meeting: a short walk somewhere public or coffee with outdoor seating?”

That’s more useful than automatically generating a supposedly charming message in someone’s voice.

**3. Turn the conversation into a shared plan.**

Either person can tap **“Make a plan.”**

They suggest:
- A date format: coffee, a public walk, lunch.
- A rough budget.
- A couple of possible times.
- A public meeting place.

The other person can **accept, suggest a change, or decline without pressure**. Nothing is booked or shared externally automatically. No home addresses or live location required.

**That creates a coherent journey: profile → meaningful conversation → agreed plan.**

## Why I would choose this

The individual ingredients aren’t unprecedented, and **I wouldn’t claim nobody else offers them**. The opportunity is to make this journey unusually simple, thoughtful and central to Freeborn.

“More filters” is easy to copy. A reputation for **helping compatible people actually meet respectfully** is harder to build—and more valuable.

But one feature will not guarantee dominance. **Reliability, enough relevant people, safety and consistent execution matter just as much.**

## What I would finish before building it

Based on the issues we’ve been working through—not a fresh production audit:

1. **Signup and onboarding:** people can finish, recover from errors and sign back in.
2. **Discovery:** working profiles, honest empty states, dependable preferences.
3. **Likes → matches → chat:** the complete loop works between two real test accounts.
4. **Notifications:** people reliably know when someone responds.
5. **Safety and privacy:** block, report, unmatch and deletion work; resolve the disclosed verification-data problems.

**A signature feature won’t save a broken core loop.**

## My recommendation

**Finish the core, then build a small Common Ground pilot—not an AI wingman, social feed, video platform or ten new features.** Start with a few prompts and a mutual date-plan card inside existing chat. Keep your four bottom tabs unchanged.

Measure whether people have more **two-way conversations and mutually accepted plans**, then ask whether those plans actually helped. Don’t mistake extra taps for successful dates.

**The direction I’d back is: Freeborn helps people turn shared values into something real.** That’s a stronger product promise than “another dating app with different filters.”
